Professional standards

A website appropriate to your practice

The design is your choice. The content is drafted carefully, so that it holds up as the professional identity of an advocate.

The Bar Council of India Rules (Rule 36, Chapter II, Part VI) set out a clear framework for advocate websites: a website is permitted, provided its content remains informational. I follow one simple principle — what belongs in a professional profile goes in, what reads as promotion stays out.

The facts, stated fully Name, enrolment number, State Bar Council, Bar Association, qualifications, practice areas and courts.
A clear disclaimer “Information only — not an advertisement or solicitation, and no advocate–client relationship is created.”
Neutral, dignified language “Contact the office”, not “book now”. An enquiry form, not a payment page.
Your site, your ownership The domain and hosting account are registered in your name. The code is handed over to you. Nothing is locked in.
Why this matters — what changed after 2024

In July 2024 the Madras High Court passed an order in P.N. Vignesh v. Bar Council of India (W.P. No. 31281 of 2019) concerning online platforms that advertise the services of advocates. The Bar Council of India then directed every State Bar Council to initiate disciplinary steps, and in 2025 issued further directions on promotional reels, videos and celebrity endorsements.

The reassuring part is that in those same orders the court repeatedly confirmed that an advocate may maintain a website — provided the content remains informational and is limited to factual particulars.

So on a site I build, you will not find:

  • Client testimonials or reviews
  • “Best advocate”, “No. 1” or “expert in” claims
  • Success rates, “cases won” or case results
  • “Free consultation”, or any paid consultation option
  • Ratings, badges or gradings

These elements look impressive, but they create risk for an advocate. The objective is not to impress — it is to protect. This is part of the service, not an add-on.

Note: I provide a website design and content service — not legal advice and not a compliance certification. Final responsibility for compliance rests with the owner of the website, and this is confirmed in writing.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is maintaining a website against Bar Council rules?
No. Rule 36 prohibits advertising and solicitation, but an advocate may maintain a website provided the content is limited to factual particulars — name, enrolment, State Bar Council, qualifications, practice areas and contact details. The elements that create difficulty, such as testimonials, “best advocate” claims, success rates and paid consultation, are not included. Final responsibility remains with you, and I confirm that in writing.
Will this bring me new clients?
I will not promise that. What a website does is this: when someone hears your name, or looks for your chamber, they find accurate, clean and credible information. That is credibility and accessibility, not lead generation. In practice, advocates with a clean profile tend to receive better enquiries, but that is not a guarantee.
I already have a Justdial or Sulekha listing.
That listing is not yours — it belongs to the platform. You appear alongside forty other advocates, your details are controlled by them, and the listing runs on their terms. Your own site is your own property. There is no need to remove the listing, but you should have a place of your own.
Who will write the content? I do not have the time.
That is the core of the service. You speak for thirty minutes; I write the biography, practice areas and disclaimer, and you only correct them. That is why the package is ₹11,999 rather than ₹4,999 — I am doing your part of the work.
In whose name will the domain and website be held?
In yours. The domain registrar and hosting account are registered in your name and email; I hold manager access only. The site code is handed over to you as well. If you ever choose not to continue with me, you lose nothing.
How long does it take?
The free sample within 48 hours, and five working days once the sample is approved. Complex setups or multiple pages may take longer, and that is stated upfront.
I already have an old website. Can it be improved instead?
Yes. I will begin with a free audit covering mobile speed, the content, anything that reads as unprofessional, and what currently appears on Google. You then decide whether to rebuild or to improve what exists.
What about changes later?
Two revision rounds are included after launch. The Care Plan (₹600 per month) includes two small changes each month. Without the Care Plan, each change is ₹500. All of this is stated in advance.
Will you also set up a Google Business Profile?
Yes, but only where there is a genuine office that clients can visit. Google does not accept virtual offices for legal services and will suspend such a profile. Verification is conducted by Google, so I make a best effort and cannot guarantee it. This is an optional add-on, not part of the core package.
