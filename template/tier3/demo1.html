FAQ

Before the first conversation.

Answers to common questions about the work, documents, fees and how to get started.

Speak to the firm
Which matters does the firm handle?

The main practices are property and civil disputes, divorce and family matters, and commercial and business law. Criminal representation, with a focus on bail and anticipatory bail, and matters before specialist courts and tribunals are also handled.

Can I consult before filing a case?

Yes. Advice is available on document review, legal notices, risk assessment, negotiation and dispute strategy before proceedings begin.

Can you verify property documents before I purchase?

Yes. Title verification examines the ownership chain, encumbrance records and supporting documents, and results in a written legal opinion. Share the property location and whether an advance, agreement or registration is approaching.

Do you assist NRIs, OCIs and clients living elsewhere?

Yes. Start with a remote discussion and the available records. You will be told what work is needed locally, how documents are handled and what authorisation or attendance is required.

What should I bring to the first consultation?

Keep the relevant agreements, deeds, notices, correspondence, pleadings and court orders ready, along with any hearing date or deadline. For an arrest or bail concern, give the police-station details and any FIR or remand information.

How are professional fees agreed?

Scope of work, professional fees and engagement terms are discussed before work begins, based on the nature of the dispute, the documents, the present stage and the representation required.

Can you represent me before the Supreme Court or a national tribunal?

Such matters are assessed individually. Please share the order, notice or case papers and any hearing or limitation deadline. Supreme Court filing involves an Advocate-on-Record where applicable.

How do I choose the right lawyer for my case?

Look for experience in the type of dispute and the forum handling it. Ask how your records will be assessed, what steps are proposed, who will handle the work, how updates are given and how fees are agreed.

Where is your office, and when can I visit?

The principal office is at [Office Address], [City]. Please call before visiting so a suitable consultation time can be arranged. If the matter is urgent, mention the hearing, deadline or immediate risk at the start of the call.
